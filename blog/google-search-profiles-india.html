Google Search profiles in India: who qualifies, how to set one up, and what to do if you don't

Google now lets Indian creators, founders and publishers with 10,000 followers build a page on Google they control. Here's what it shows, how to set it up in seven steps, and how to prepare if you don't qualify yet.

EvangelineMarketing Head, GreyLeads

Search your own name or your company's name on Google, and what comes up is mostly out of your hands: a LinkedIn page, an old news mention, maybe a directory listing. Google Search profiles give some people a page on Google that they control, and since October they've been available in India.

The short answer: a Google Search profile is a free page on Google that brings together your bio, links, articles, videos and social posts, and lets people follow you so your content is more likely to show up in their Google Discover feed. In India you can create one if you're 18 or older and have at least 10,000 followers or subscribers on YouTube, Instagram, X or TikTok. It won't improve your rankings, but it gives founders, experts and publishers more say over how they appear on Google. Here's who qualifies, how to set one up, and what to do if you don't qualify yet.

What a Google Search profile is

Google launched Search profiles in the US in June 2026 as a dedicated, shareable page where publishers and creators can show their latest work. According to Google's help page, a profile shows:

  • Your name, bio and social links.
  • Content from the accounts you link, which Google adds automatically. New content usually appears within 24 hours.
  • A way for people to follow you. Google says following a source makes it more likely that its content appears in that person's Discover feed.

People reach it through a "View Search Profile" link under your knowledge panel in Search, by tapping your name above a Discover card, or through a direct link you can share. Google also says claiming a profile may lead to a knowledge panel being created for eligible creators and publishers.

What's changed for India

Two changes this year make Search profiles relevant to Indian businesses:

  • India is now included. On 6 October, Robby Stein, Google's VP of Product for Search, announced that creators and publishers in India and Canada can set up a profile, with Brazil coming later, as Search Engine Roundtable reported. Google's help page now lists Canada, India and the United States.
  • The follower bar is much lower. At launch you needed 100,000 followers on YouTube, Instagram or X. Google lowered it to 35,000 in August, then to 10,000 across YouTube, Instagram, X and TikTok, as it explained in a September update.

At 10,000 followers, plenty of Bangalore and Chennai founders, doctors, chefs, coaches, architects and niche publishers now qualify.

Who should set one up, and who shouldn't bother yet

A Search profile is worth the hour it takes if you or your brand already publish regularly on an eligible platform and people search for you by name. That includes founders known for their content, consultants and experts who post on YouTube or Instagram, restaurants and studios with active Instagram accounts, and media brands and newsletters.

Two things to keep in mind:

  • LinkedIn doesn't count. Google's list of eligible platforms is YouTube, Instagram, X and TikTok. Many B2B founders in Bangalore have their audience on LinkedIn, so they may not qualify even with a large following.
  • It isn't a Google Business Profile. Your Business Profile is the listing that shows your shop, clinic or office on Maps and in local search. A Search profile is about a person, creator or publisher and their content. Most local businesses should finish their Business Profile first; our Google Business Profile checklist covers that.

How to set up a Google Search profile in India: 7 steps

1. Check you're eligible

You need at least one account with 10,000 or more followers or subscribers on YouTube, Instagram, X or TikTok, and you must be 18 or older. Your content also has to meet Google's policies for user-generated content on Search.

2. Look for an existing profile first

Go to profile.google.com, or search your name on Google and look for "View Search Profile" under your knowledge panel. If a profile already exists, claim it rather than creating a second one.

3. Decide which identity the profile is for

Google lets one Google account manage up to 10 profiles, one for each brand or identity. Decide whether you need a profile for yourself as a founder or expert, one for the brand, or both. Use the Google account you'll keep long term, ideally a company account you control, not a personal account of someone who might leave.

4. Create the profile with your strongest account

On the setup page, sign in and connect at least one eligible account. If you have a YouTube channel, Google says to create the profile with the channel that has the most subscribers, then link the others. Your handle defaults to the handle of your most-followed platform, and you can change it later. Then click Create Profile.

5. Link only the accounts that belong to that identity

Google warns that it may refuse accounts that aren't relevant to the profile, even if you can sign in to them. Keep the founder's personal accounts on the founder's profile and the brand's accounts on the brand's profile.

6. Write the bio and links carefully

Changes to your name, bio or social links go to Google for approval and stay "Pending" until reviewed, so get them right the first time. In two or three plain sentences, say who you are, what you do and where you are, for example "Founder of a Bengaluru architecture studio. Writes about small-home design." Link to your website, and make sure your website's About or team page links back to the same accounts, so everything points to the same identity. Our branding team can help you write a consistent bio across every platform.

7. Keep publishing, and measure what changes

A profile is only as useful as the content flowing into it, because Google pulls in your new posts automatically. Keep posting on your linked accounts and on your website. To see whether anything changes, check the Discover report in Google Search Console if your website gets Discover traffic, and track branded searches and enquiries that mention finding you on Google. Google's help page is clear that a profile doesn't directly affect ranking in Search.

If you don't qualify yet

Most of what makes a Search profile useful comes from Google understanding clearly who you are. You can work on that now:

  • Complete your Google Business Profile if you have a physical location or serve a local area.
  • Give your website a proper About page and team pages with consistent names, roles, photos and links to your social accounts, plus structured data that describes your organisation and people. Our SEO and AEO team sets this up, and it also helps AI tools describe you accurately, as we explain in how to get your brand recommended by ChatGPT.
  • Pick one eligible platform where your customers actually spend time, usually YouTube or Instagram for Indian consumer brands, and post on it consistently.

Example: a Bangalore interior design studio, step by step

This is an illustrative example: the studio is made up, and it's only here to show how the steps fit together.

The studio's founder is the public face of the business. The studio has an Instagram account showing finished homes, the founder posts design advice on YouTube, and the studio has a Google Business Profile for its office. Clients often search the founder's name before they call.

  • Step 1: the founder checks the numbers. The studio's Instagram account is over Google's threshold. The founder's YouTube channel isn't yet.
  • Step 2: searching the founder's name and the studio's name shows no existing Search profile.
  • Step 3: they decide on one profile for the studio for now, managed from the studio's own Google account, and plan a founder profile once the YouTube channel qualifies.
  • Steps 4 and 5: the profile is created with the studio's Instagram account. The founder's YouTube channel isn't linked, because it belongs to a different identity.
  • Step 6: the bio says what the studio designs and that it's in Bengaluru, and links to the website. The website's About page is updated to link to the same Instagram account and to the Business Profile.
  • Step 7: the studio keeps posting finished projects, and the team adds "How did you find us?" to the enquiry form.

What to expect. The profile doesn't guarantee more enquiries or better rankings, and Google decides when it appears. What it does is give people who search for the studio, or come across it in Discover, a page the studio controls, with its latest work. The studio should measure branded searches, Discover clicks in Search Console if it gets any, and how many enquiries mention Google, comparing the months before and after.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Search profile available in India?

Yes. Google opened Search profiles to creators and publishers in India and Canada in October 2026, and its help page lists Canada, India and the United States as supported countries.

How many followers do I need for a Google Search profile?

At least 10,000 followers or subscribers on one of YouTube, Instagram, X or TikTok, according to Google's help page. You also need to be 18 or older.

Is a Search profile the same as a Google Business Profile?

No. A Google Business Profile is the listing for a business location on Maps and local search. A Search profile is a page for a creator, expert or publisher that gathers their content and lets people follow them.

Will a Google Search profile help my SEO?

Not directly. Google says creating a profile doesn't affect ranking in Search. It can help people find and follow your content, including in Discover, and gives you more control over how you appear when people search your name.

The short version

  1. Search profiles are now available in India, and the bar is 10,000 followers on YouTube, Instagram, X or TikTok.
  2. LinkedIn followers don't count, and a Search profile isn't a Google Business Profile.
  3. Claim an existing profile if there is one, create it with your strongest account and link only relevant accounts.
  4. Write the bio carefully, because changes need Google's approval.
  5. Judge it on branded searches, Discover clicks and enquiries, not rankings.
