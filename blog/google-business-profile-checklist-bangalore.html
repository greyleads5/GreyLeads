Google Business Profile optimisation in Bangalore: a 12-point checklist for more calls and leads

Most local enquiries start on Google Search or Maps. Here's how to set up your profile so the right people in Bengaluru find you, trust you and contact you, including the WhatsApp chat button.

EvangelineMarketing Head, GreyLeads

When someone in HSR Layout searches for a physiotherapist near them, or types "interior designer Whitefield" into Google, the first thing they usually see isn't a website. It's a map and a short list of business profiles. Whoever looks the most relevant, the closest and the most trusted gets the call.

The short answer: Google Business Profile optimisation comes down to accurate details, the right category, a steady flow of genuine reviews that you reply to, and making it easy to contact you by phone, WhatsApp or your website. You can't buy your way up. Google's own help page on how to improve your local ranking says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. This checklist covers what to fix, in order, with examples from Bengaluru, and why it's worth doing before Diwali on Sunday, 8 November.

How Google decides which local businesses to show

Google says local results are based mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what the person searched for. Complete, detailed information helps here.
  • Distance: how far your business is from the searcher, or from the location in their search.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is. Google lists reviews, ratings and links from other websites as signals.

You can't move your office closer to every customer. You can make your profile clearly relevant and steadily more prominent. That's what the checklist below does. In a city as spread out as Bengaluru, where a customer in Yelahanka rarely wants a dentist in Electronic City, getting relevance right for your own part of the city matters more than trying to rank everywhere.

The 12-point Google Business Profile checklist

1. Claim and verify the profile, under a company account

Google says verified profiles are more likely to appear in results. Just as important: check who owns the profile. It's surprisingly common for it to sit under a former employee's personal Gmail. Make sure the owner is a Google account the business controls, and add your agency or team as managers rather than sharing the password.

2. Use your real business name, and nothing else

Google's guidelines for representing your business say the name should match what customers see on your signage, website and stationery. Taglines, phone numbers, URLs and location or service keywords don't belong there. "Sharma Dental Clinic" is right. "Sharma Dental Clinic | Best Dentist in Koramangala" breaks the guidelines.

3. Pick the most specific primary category

Your primary category tells Google what you are. The guidelines ask you to use as few categories as possible, each as specific as possible, and not to use categories as keywords or as a list of services. A clinic that mainly does braces should check whether a more specific category fits better than a general one. Add secondary categories only for things that are genuinely a core part of the business.

4. Get the address or service area exactly right

  • Have a storefront or office customers visit? Use the precise real-world address. Put the floor or unit number in address line 2. Google says P.O. boxes and virtual offices aren't eligible, and you should have one profile per location.
  • Go to customers instead? A pest-control or home-repair business should set up as a service-area business, hide the address and list the areas it serves. Google's guidance is that service areas generally shouldn't stretch much beyond about two hours' drive from your base. "All of Bengaluru" makes sense. "All of Karnataka" doesn't.

5. Set your hours, and add Diwali special hours now

Wrong hours cost you twice: a wasted trip for the customer, and often a bad review. Google recommends keeping regular and special hours up to date and using special hours for holidays. If you're closing, or opening shorter hours, around Diwali on 8 November, add those as special hours this week. Do the same for any year-end closures in December.

6. Turn on WhatsApp chat

For Indian customers, WhatsApp is often the easiest way to make a first enquiry. Google's help page on chatting with customers from your Business Profile explains the steps:

  1. Open your Business Profile and choose Edit profile, then Contact.
  2. Next to Chat, select edit and choose WhatsApp from the drop-down.
  3. Paste your WhatsApp click-to-chat link and save.

Two details from Google: chat is only available in select regions, and if you add both WhatsApp and text messaging, customers will only see the text option. So pick WhatsApp alone if that's where your team answers. In early October, Search Engine Roundtable reported WhatsApp buttons showing in Google's local panel, in place of or alongside the other action buttons. Google hasn't announced it formally, but it's another reason to set chat up correctly now.

One rule before you switch it on: someone must reply within minutes during business hours. A greeting message and quick replies in WhatsApp Business help, and if leads arrive faster than your team can answer, simple lead automation can route each chat to the right person and log it.

7. Describe your services in your customers' words

Fill in the services and products sections, the business description and the attributes that apply to you. Write the way customers search: "home loan for self-employed", "kids' dental check-up", "modular kitchen design". This is the relevance signal Google talks about, and it's also what convinces a person to tap Call instead of the next profile. Keep it factual. No ALL CAPS, no lists of localities.

8. Add real photos and short videos

Google encourages photos and videos that show what you offer. Use your real premises, team, signage (so people recognise the entrance) and finished work. Skip stock images; customers can tell. Add a few new photos every month rather than fifty on one day and nothing after.

9. Ask every customer for a review, the right way

Reviews feed prominence, and they're what most people read before they call. But Google's policy on prohibited content is strict about how you get them:

  • No discounts, freebies or payment in exchange for a review, or for removing a negative one.
  • Don't ask only the happy customers. Selectively asking for positive reviews isn't allowed.
  • No reviews from employees, and don't give staff review quotas.

What works: send your profile's review link on WhatsApp or SMS right after the job is done or the appointment ends, to every customer, with one line asking for honest feedback. A steady trickle every week beats a burst followed by months of silence.

10. Reply to every review, especially the bad ones

Google recommends responding to reviews, and a good reply is as much for the next reader as for the reviewer. Thank people by name, mention what they came in for, and keep it short. For a negative review, acknowledge it, don't argue in public, and give a direct number or email to sort it out. Avoid the same copy-pasted reply on every review.

11. Send website clicks to a page that converts

Link to the page that best matches what people find you for, not just your homepage, and add UTM tags (for example utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp) so GA4 shows Business Profile visits separately. If people land and leave, the problem may be the page, not the profile. Our guide on why website traffic doesn't turn into leads covers the usual fixes, and our website team builds pages for local enquiries.

12. Track calls, chats and directions every month

Your profile's performance metrics show how people found you and what they did: calls, website clicks, direction requests and, once it's on, chat activity. Note them on the first of every month. If calls dip while impressions hold steady, look at your reviews, photos and hours. If impressions fall, look at categories and competitors. This turns the profile from a one-time setup into a lead source you manage.

Five mistakes to avoid

  • Two profiles for the same location, often one created by a previous agency. Merge or remove the duplicate.
  • A phone number that belongs to someone who has left, or that goes unanswered at lunch.
  • Locality names stuffed into the business name or description.
  • Offering a discount "for a 5-star review". It breaks Google's policy and those reviews can be removed.
  • Setting everything up once and not touching it for a year.
Your Business Profile is often your first impression. Treat it like a shopfront you sweep every week.

A 20-minute weekly routine

  1. Reply to every new review.
  2. Answer any unread chats and check that calls are being picked up.
  3. Add one or two new photos of real work.
  4. Check hours for the coming week, festivals included.
  5. Once a month, note calls, chats, website clicks and direction requests.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay Google to rank higher on Google Maps?

No. Google says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Rankings depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, so the way up is a complete, accurate profile and genuine reviews.

How do I add WhatsApp to my Google Business Profile?

Go to Edit profile, then Contact, edit Chat, choose WhatsApp and paste your click-to-chat link. Chat is available in select regions only, and if you add both WhatsApp and text messaging, customers see only the text option.

Can I offer a discount to customers who leave a review?

No. Google's policy doesn't allow payment, discounts or free goods or services in exchange for reviews, and such reviews can be removed. Ask every customer for honest feedback instead.

Should I add "Bangalore" or keywords to my business name?

Only if it's genuinely part of your real-world name, as shown on your signage and website. Google's guidelines don't allow adding location or service keywords to the business name.

How many categories should I choose?

As few as possible. Pick the most specific primary category that describes what your business is, and add secondary categories only for core parts of the business, not for every service.

The short version

  1. Google ranks local businesses on relevance, distance and prominence, and you can't pay to rank higher.
  2. Use your real name, the most specific category and an exact address or service area.
  3. Add Diwali special hours this week, and switch on WhatsApp chat if your team can answer quickly.
  4. Ask every customer for a review, never with an incentive, and reply to all of them.
  5. Send clicks to a page that converts and check your profile metrics every month.
